Research

Dam Breach Studio

Ongoing, 2026 – present · independent research while a Research Assistant at CARD

Most GLOF studies, including my own first paper, take the breach width and formation time from regression equations fitted to engineered dams. I wanted to see what happens if you compute the breach instead, so I started writing a model that follows the whole chain: the trigger (piping, overtopping, or an avalanche into the lake), the erosion of the dam, and the outflow hydrograph.

It has two engines. The 1D one is written in TypeScript and runs in the browser. The 2D one is a finite-volume shallow-water solver in Python and NumPy, where the breach location and shape come out of the solution. Some of what's in it:

  • overtopping and piping failure, with pipe enlargement, roof collapse and headcut migration;
  • cohesive erosion (Wan & Fell, Hanson JET) and Meyer-Peter–Müller bed load;
  • moraine bank collapse, and VAW impulse waves for avalanche- or landslide-triggered floods;
  • hydrograph export to CSV, JSON and Excel for routing in HEC-RAS.

It's research code and still changing. The verification suite passes 67 of 67 checks, and it reproduces the Teton Dam (1976) peak discharge within 15%, though the timing is still off. I keep the open problems listed in the repository.

Built with TypeScript, React, Python, NumPy · Code on GitHub

GLOF modelling of Lower Barun Lake

Published in Discover Civil Engineering (Springer Nature), February 2025 · open access

Lower Barun Lake sits below Makalu in eastern Nepal, and the Arun-3 hydropower project is being built downstream. With five fellow students and our supervisor, I modelled what an outburst flood from the lake could do. I built the HEC-RAS 2D model and made the inundation maps.

We generated breach hydrographs with FLDWAV for avalanche-triggered failures and for a cascade from Upper Barun Lake, mapped avalanche-prone slopes with GIS and AHP, simulated the avalanche itself in RAMMS, and routed the flood over a 12.5 m ALOS PALSAR DEM. A full breach gave a peak discharge of 51,231 m³/s, with water reaching Arun-3 in about two hours and a peak depth of 49.12 m there. A 25% breach peaked at 9,014 m³/s.

Tools HEC-RAS, FLDWAV, RAMMS, QGIS, Landsat · Read the paper · 3,398 accesses and 6 citations on Springer as of 3 Nov 2026

Landslide susceptibility along the Karnali Highway

    
2025 – 2026 · preprint on EarthArXiv, manuscript under review

    
The Karnali Highway is the main road into Karnali Province, and landslides block it every monsoon. With Ayush Karki, Amrit Bhusal and Akhilesh Kumar Karna, I mapped susceptibility over a corridor of about 2,155 km², using 460 mapped landslides.

    
We wanted to know how much the answer depends on the mapping unit, so we ran the same models on 3 m, 15 m and 25 m grids and on slope units from r.slopeunits. Random Forest, XGBoost and SVM were tuned with Bayesian optimisation and tested with spatially blocked cross-validation, and SHAP showed which factors drove each map. We also flagged the areas where the models are extrapolating, using Mahalanobis distance and how much the models agree. The best AUC was 0.995 on grid units and 0.87 on slope units.

    
Tools Python, scikit-learn, XGBoost, SHAP, QGIS, GRASS GIS · Preprint · Code and data
